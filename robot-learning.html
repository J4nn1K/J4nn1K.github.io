TL;DR I'm finetuning Octo to new observation and action spaces for ALOHA/ACT's Transfer Cube task.

This year between christmas and NYE I gave myself four days to figure out a way to demonstrate why robot learning is amazing. This page shows is the result. I hope you enjoy!

This demo is combining three major breakthrougs which happened in robotics in 2023. A low-cost open source manipulation setup [1], the first large scale robotic dataset [2] and a powerful generalist control policy [3]. I want to show how easy it will be to teach robots skills in the future.

Finetuning

to a new observation space (single camera + proprio) and new action space (bimanual) using a simulated ALOHA cube handover dataset


Task Definition

For the simulated task Transfer Cube, the right arm needs to first pick up the red cube lying on the table, then place it inside the gripper of the other arm. Due to the small clearance between the cube and the left gripper (around 1cm), small errors could result in collisions and task failure [1].



Dataset

simulated ALOHA cube handover dataset aloha_sim_cube_scripted_dataset 48 demonstrations

Training

The model for 25000 steps with a batch size of 128 (octo-small) and 64 (octo-base) using a TPU v3-8 pod, which took around 3 hours and cost around $20 per run. The training history can be found here: https://wandb.ai/j4nn1k/octo-finetuning

(Success rate and smoothness of Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) policies can improve way after loss plateaus.)


Results

Octo-Small (27M) after 25000 steps: around 70% success rate. Inference on a RTX 3070

Related Work

Excellent work that this project is based on:

[1] Learning Fine-Grained Bimanual Manipulation with Low-Cost Hardware introduces ALOHA and ACT. ALOHA is a low-cost open-source hardware system for bimanual teleoperation. ACT (Action Chunking with Transformers) predicts a sequence of actions ("an action chunk") instead of a single action like standard Behavior Cloning.

[2] Open X-Embodiment: Robotic Learning Datasets and RT-X Models introduces the largest open-source real robot dataset to date.

[3] Octo: An Open-Source Generalist Robot Policy introduces an ongoing effort for building open-source, widely applicable generalist policies for robotic manipulation. The Octo model is a transformer-based diffusion policy, pretrained on 800k robot episodes from the Open X-Embodiment dataset.
